    What do the symbols mean?

    The middle of the radar is straight above you and the edge is the horizon, so a high object sits near the middle and a low one near the edge. The rings mark 30° and 60° above the horizon. North is the red N.

    • ✈︎Aircraft. The nose points the way it is flying.
    • Satellite. Blue means sunlit, grey means in the Earth's shadow, and a bright white one can be seen with the eye.
    • A larger diamond with a name: a space station, the ISS or Tiangong.
    • The Sun.
    • The Moon.
    • A planet. A bigger dot is a brighter planet.
    • A bright star.
    • A meteor shower, such as the Leonids. The plus is the radiant: the point in the sky that the shooting stars seem to come from. Look at the whole sky, not only at the plus, because the trails appear everywhere.
    • A white ring: the object you picked.
    • A dashed line: where the object you picked is going (a flight's next minutes, a satellite's orbit, the Sun, Moon, planets and stars over the next hours, with a dot each hour), or a space station pass you chose in the list.
