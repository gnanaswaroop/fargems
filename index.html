How it works

  1. Hold the phone flat. A radar map shows the whole sky, with north always at the top and a wedge for the way you face.
  2. Spot something. Only flights close enough and high enough to see by eye are drawn. Tap one for its details.
  3. Raise the phone. Point it at the sky and the camera view labels what you are looking at, and an arrow guides you to the one you picked.

About FarGems

FarGems is a sky map for your location. It shows what is above you right now, drawn as a radar-style view with a sweep you can turn off.

What it shows

  • Aircraft you can actually see from where you stand (25 km away by default, and you can change that), with altitude, speed, heading and where they are flying to.
  • Satellites, including the ISS, Tiangong and about 100 of the brightest, with their next passes.
  • The Sun, Moon and planets, with rise and set times and the Moon's phase.
  • Bright stars and the radiants of active meteor showers.
  • Cloud cover for your area, when available.

Layers and radar

Open Layers in the sky view to switch each layer on or off: the object layers, altitude rings and compass, names, and the radar sweep. The sweep is off if your device asks for reduced motion. With the compass on, north stays at the top and a wedge shows the way you face.

Camera view

Raise your phone to point at the sky and the camera view opens, labelling what is there. Hold it flat or tilted to read and you stay on the radar. The camera picture never leaves your phone.

Where the data comes from

Aircraft
adsb.lol, airplanes.live and OpenSky, with flight routes from adsbdb
Satellites
CelesTrak orbit data, with positions calculated in your browser
Sun, Moon, planets, stars
Calculated in your browser with astronomy-engine
Meteor showers
A built-in table of approximate dates and radiants
Clouds
Open-Meteo

Privacy

Your location and compass stay on your device. The app sends your coordinates only to the public data services above, to fetch data for your area. Aircraft requests pass through a small relay that forwards only a rounded area to the feeds and stores nothing about you. There are no accounts and no tracking.

Limits

  • Aircraft come from community feeds, so some aircraft are missing or delayed.
  • The compass is usually 5–20° off, and results vary by phone.
  • Meteor shower dates and radiants are approximate, and individual meteors can't be tracked.
